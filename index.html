Before you start - safety first

HSE
Stay off the roof and off ladders. Falls from roof edges and through fragile roofs and rooflights are the main causes of death and injury in roof work. Treat all roofs as fragile until a competent person has confirmed they are not. (UK HSE Roof work, UK HSE Fragile surfaces)
  • Look from the ground, from across the street, or from an upstairs window. Binoculars or your phone's zoom help. (Professional practice)
  • Roof space: enter only if there is safe, lit, boarded access and you have permission. Never step between joists or onto the ceiling. Do not touch cables. (Professional practice)
  • Water near light fittings or electrics: do not touch. A qualified electrician must inspect wet electrical equipment. (CDC Electrical)
  • After a storm, keep away from damaged roofs, loose tiles and fallen materials. (Professional practice)

Roof problems often show only after rain. Check the same places during or soon after each heavy rain, and log what changed. A pattern over several storms is strong evidence for a roofer.

    What this tool will not do

    LIMITS
    • It will not tell you where a leak comes from. Water can travel a long way inside a roof before it shows.
    • It never asks you to go onto a roof, use a ladder, or walk in a roof space without safe boarded access.
    • It does not predict how long a roof will last and does not replace a roofer, building surveyor or structural engineer.

    Standards & Measurements

    REFERENCES

    Universal guidance used in this tool

    • Roof work risk: the main causes of death and injury are falling from roof edges or openings, through fragile roofs and through fragile rooflights. All roofs should be treated as fragile until a competent person confirms they are not. (UK HSE Roof work, UK HSE Fragile surfaces)
    • What a home inspector looks at: roof-covering materials, gutters, downspouts, vents, flashing, skylights, chimney and other penetrations, and reports observed indications of active roof leaks. Eaves, soffits and fascia. Insulation and ventilation of attics, reporting the general absence of either. Inspectors are not required to walk on roofs. (InterNACHI Standards of Practice 3.1, 3.2, 3.9)
    • Flashing plays a critical role in excluding water and diverting it out of the building. (EPA Moisture Control Guidance, 2013)
    • Drain water away: drain rain and snowmelt away from the building. Make sure the ground slopes away from the foundation. Clean and repair gutters regularly. (EPA Moisture Control Guidance, EPA Brief Guide)
    • Moisture sources: leaking roofs, and gutters and downspouts that direct water into or under a building. (EPA Mold Course, Chapter 2)
    • After a leak: dry wet materials within 24-48 hours; in most cases mould will not grow. Do not paint over mould. (EPA Brief Guide)
    • Wet electrics: a qualified electrician must inspect before power is restored. (CDC Electrical)
    Country-specific rules: building regulations, roofing standards, planning rules for roofs and listed or heritage buildings, and seller disclosure duties differ between countries. This tool does not state local requirements. Verify with your local authority, conveyancer or a registered roofing professional.

    Items marked Professional practice reflect general inspection and HSE practice. No single public standard covers them, so no standard is claimed.

      Before you start: how your information is saved

      Your information is saved on this device only.

      Save a backup file regularly.

      Where is my information saved?

      Inside this browser, on this device. Nothing is sent to The Snag Tag or anyone else. That keeps it private, but it also means you are the only one who can keep a copy safe.

      Why could my information seem to disappear?
      • You open the tool in a different browser, a private/incognito window, or on another device.
      • You open a different copy of the tool (for example a downloaded file instead of your link).
      • Your browsing history or site data gets cleared.
      • Safari (iPhone, iPad and Mac) can delete saved information if you don't open the tool for about 7 days.
      • The tool is open in two tabs at the same time. Always use one tab.
      How do I keep my information safe?
      • Press Save a backup now after every session where you change something.
      • Keep the backup file somewhere safe, like your cloud drive or a USB stick.
      • Always open the tool from the same link, in the same browser.
      How do I get my information back, or use another device?

      Open the tool, press Restore a backup and choose your latest backup file. Everything comes back as it was when you saved it.
